# Honest review of draft v0 (neighbours-draft.pdf, 2026-10-24)

## Big picture
A clear, correct, *pedagogical* walk through why discrete spacetime and Lorentz invariance fight, rediscovering
causal-set results (links, infinite valency, BHS dilemma, Number = Volume, growth) from a different starting point
(crystal + quantum blur). As a paper: no new result yet. As a notebook checkpoint: solid.

## Strengths
- Calculations are elementary and correct (sliver area, rapidity bands, link count 1/unit rapidity, Schild orbit,
  3+1 link count, chain blur ln N with numerics).
- Clean unifying slogan: "normalisable ⟂ boost-invariant" (known content, good explanation).
- Honest tagging; limitations stated.

## Candidate original content (ranked)
1. Finite universe as rapidity cutoff (η_max ≈ ln(2L/ℓ_P) ≈ 142 vs observed 27). Most interesting; unfalsifiable as stated; may exist.
2. Point budget (expansion creates, gravity destroys, distributed sink). Interesting picture; slicing-dependent.
3. Blur from collective zero-point modes (toy chain). Standard physics, new use; weak link to Lorentz problem.
Everything else is known content in new words.

## Main flaws
- No dynamics: nothing yields Einstein's equations or Newtonian gravity.
- Background dependence: blur, flows, density presuppose a background space.
- No falsifiable prediction.
- Mostly 1+1; 3+1 is qualitatively different.
- Literature searches shallow.

## What would make it a real paper (any one)
- A covariant point budget that reproduces Newtonian gravity in some limit.
- A prediction from finite valency in 3+1 (e.g. effect on Sorkin's nonlocal d'Alembertian in a finite universe).
- Show that random point creation gives Sorkin's √N fluctuations *and* a definite Λ(t) to test against data.
