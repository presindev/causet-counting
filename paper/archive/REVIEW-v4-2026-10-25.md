# Honest review of draft v4 (draft-v4.pdf, 2026-10-25, session 5)

## What changed from v3
- **Cutoff section:** new subsection on Sorkin's 1+1 □ with the cutoff. A finite past keeps □ exact for every wavelength. The integral is
  only conditionally convergent, so the regulator matters: a rapidity wedge gives spurious −1/−2 answers, while a finite past diamond gives 0 and 4pq.
  This agrees with Aslanbeigi–Saravani–Sorkin's analytic continuation. The session-4 "abrupt failure once λ > L" is now listed as a dropped path.
- **Quantum section:** the N = 100 stress-quartile pilot is replaced by A1 (10⁶ orders at N = 200, layer-count record). Quantum suppresses
  few-layer universes, but P_q ∝ n_r² e^{−β²σ_r²} explains all of it: a variance effect.
- **N = 400:** a preliminary hot/cold hysteresis paragraph (laptop only; the cluster run is in progress).
- Abstract, discussion, limitations and next steps updated; refs ASS14, Loomis–Carlip, Caravelli–Markopoulou added.

## What is (maybe) ours
- As in v3: rectangle identity, depth-drift curvature, calibrated manifold detector.
- The finite past as the physical regulator of the □ integral, with the "¼ per null edge" bookkeeping. The math is elementary and the conclusion
  matches ASS14, so the novelty is small: an interpretation for our C3, not a new result.
- The variance formula for sampled record probabilities. It's a Gaussian cumulant statement and very likely known in sign-problem literature.

## Main weaknesses
1. Still 1+1 only. The cutoff result in 3+1 is not computed, and C6 needs 3+1.
2. The paper now has **two negative results** (no cutoff phenomenology, quantum effect = variance) and no positive physics claim beyond the toolkit.
   That's honest, but the storyline should say plainly that the next step (layer-by-layer dynamics) is where anything new would have to come from.
3. The N = 400 paragraph rests on 100-sweep laptop runs. It must be replaced by the cluster result before any further use.
4. The diamond regulator was centred in the cosmological frame. A wave boosted relative to it was covered by varying p, but a boosted *diamond*
   with a fixed wave wasn't checked separately (it should be equivalent by Lorentz invariance of the operator).
5. Choices still not derived: ε = 0.21, the records, the detector thresholds.

## What would upgrade it
- The N = 400 result (where is β_c; is the two-layer order the true crystal?).
- A2: anything that the transfer-matrix formulation can say beyond β ≈ 0.2.
- The 3+1 version of the cutoff check (expected to agree with ASS14's smooth IR behaviour).

## Addendum (end of session 5): Section 5 "Growing it as a film"
- New section with A2: thick height layers fail (memory ≈ 40 % of the age, breaks clock independence); the clock-free per-birth cut by
  events-between works at fixed n_cut = 10 (≈ 0.06 σ_S at every N); the thick present is finite iff the universe has no event horizon.
- **Strengths:** each claim is a direct, cheap measurement with a validation (growth sum = action to 1e-10; m = ∞ reproduces exact sums).
  The event-horizon criterion is clean and easy to state.
- **Weaknesses:** (1) only the amplitude is truncated, never the counting measure, so this isn't a dynamics yet. (2) The "state" is defined in hindsight
  (who *will* need x), a lower bound; a predictive state could only be larger, which strengthens the negative results but leaves the positive one
  (static closed universe) optimistic. (3) Only n_cut = 10 and 1+1. (4) No literature search on the thick-present / event-horizon result; quantum
  sequential growth people may know it in another language. (5) The "universe as an efficient computer" framing is a motivation, not a result.
- **Overall state of the draft:** a coherent notebook paper with one positive toolkit (counting geometry) and three honest negatives
  (cutoff invisible; quantum effect = variance; no finite-state film under acceleration). It has no prediction and no 3+1 content.
  Not ready for anyone outside the notebook; a good map of where we stand.
